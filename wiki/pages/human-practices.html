Our beginnings

Everything starts with a necessity, only initiative is required to make a change. Since the beginning of our project, we had our sights set on working with cows, these beautiful animals that provide us with milk, a highly nutritious food for many1.While investigating current issues in our community, we came across mastitis a bacterial infection that results in swelling and pain, affecting the cow's udder.Dairy mastitis is a significant concern,as it leads to decreased milk production,changes in milk quality, andeconomic losses for dairy farmers. ,a highly common infection in dairy cows. Aureobos a non-antibiotic treatment for mastitis, strives to minimize the necessity for milk withdrawal during treatment, In this way, food waste and economic losses suffered by milk producers would be reduced. High treatment costs



Story Behind

This is the milking parlor of Felipe, a small-scale producer in Delicias, when we first arrived we had a shock of reality, cows were being milked in a small concrete room in which only four cows could be milked at the same time. Felipe mentioned that instead of using a seal to clean the cow's nipples he used chlorine, it proved effective for him and was also cheaper.



Many small-scale producers do not have access to conventional treatments, because of the elevated prices at the market, they even use everyday supplies to ensure good hygiene in their sheds. On the other hand, we have the dairy industry, which has a full stock of antibiotics when needed. However, mastitis incurs significant financial losses in the global dairy industry, amounting to an estimated 4.7B to 7.68B USD in veterinary fees and drug expenses7

Veterinary zootechnicians who manage dairy cow sheds, pointed out that mastitis represents the greatest expense in relation to cattle diseases, so no matter how expensive the treatments are, both sectors are affected economically.

Milk withdrawal



Story Behind

During our visit to the Zaragoza industry in Delicias, we had the opportunity to see a newborn calf. This sparked our curiosity and led us to ask ¿what it´s done with the milk from sick cows? They mentioned that, when mastitis is first detected at the parlor, the cow must be milked in a separate room, and the milk obtained during the infection and treatment was used in male calves, otherwise, it went to waste.



Once antibiotics are administered during the sickness, they are excreted through pee, excrement, and most importantly in milk

During our interviews, the most mentioned antibiotics employed for treating mastitis were Ceftiofur, Mastex, Cefavet, Mastijet, Enromastic, and Pencivet. The withdrawal period that every antibiotic requires reduces the overall milk production per dairy, contributing to economic losses for the producer. As we can see in Fig.8, the most common antibiotics on the market require long milk withdrawal periods except for Ceftiofur. This is really concerning not only because of the economic losses but for the food that is going to waste during the process.

There is a variety of detection methods used for SCC, during the interviews with our stakeholders they mentioned three really important methods, California Test, Wisconsin Test, and milk conductivity.

California Test

This test is the most commonly used in the industry for subclinical mastitis detection. It is a qualitative measurement that involves a reactant (Sodium Lauryl sulfate 2%, Crystal violet 0.0033%, Aqueous vehicle q.s., reagent in solution)9 and a blast of milk. How to interpret results from the test?

The results can be categorized ranging from a negative outcome where the milk and reagent maintain a liquid consistency to the highest cell count, where the milk-reagent mixture nearly solidifies, so the higher the cell presence, the more DNA concentration is released, leading to increased gelatin formation, ultimately resulting in our interpretation of the result as indicating the most pronounced level of inflammation.

Wisconsin Test

We also had an interview with Gabriela Mapes, a stakeholder in the pharmaceutical area, as she works with Zoetis a well-known global animal health company that specializes in the development and manufacturing of veterinary pharmaceuticals, vaccines, diagnostics, and various other products and services related to animal health and well-being.During our interview, she mentioned the use of WMT test for SCC, so we delved into finding out what it consisted.



The Wisconsin Mastitis Test (WMT) was created with laboratories in mind, and it's like a detective tool for milk quality. It helps us figure out the amount of somatic cells present in fresh blended milk, milk from chilling tanks, and even milk from individual cows. It is similar to the California Test (CMT) in terms of the solution it uses. But here's the cool part: instead of just giving a qualitative result like the CMT, the WMT gives us a quantitative result, that is detailed on the numbers based on how thick the milk becomes. It's like having a precise measurement to understand the milk's health.WMT consists of using a millimeter-marked tube into which you place 2 ml of milk, along with a mixture of 2 ml of CMT reagent and distilled water in equal parts, which is then mixed.

The results are linked to the milliliter markings on the tube and what they tell us about somatic cells. We've got this handy chart that helps us understand what it all means. So, if a herd scores low, for example between 3 and 12, it's doing pretty well—no major worries. But if the score is above 12, it signifies a great concern, and we've got to jump into action right away10.

Milk conductivity

Measurement of electrical conductivity (EC) is a method frequently used in dairy cows during milking in milking parlors, but especially in robotic milking as a low-cost mastitis detection method. Therefore conductivity of milk seems to hold promise as an indicator of subclinical mastitis11.

AQUI VA LAS CARD DE LOS IMPACT

As any scientific emerging project, there is potential for it to be misused or generate unintended consequences. While we aim to develop AureoBos as an alternative treatment for bovine mastitis in a responsible and beneficial way, it is important to consider certain risks in order to plan how to manage them. Inside the lab, while developing our project, the team has received proper training in order to work in a safe and responsible environment, following a series of measures and guidelines further explained in the safety section. On the other hand, some of the risks we visualize outside the lab are; the possible unregulated use of AureoBos which, without proper guidance, could lead to ineffective treatment or unintended side effects; the overreliance on it, which is important to remember that different cases of mastitis may require different approaches, and a balanced approach to treatment is necessary; an unauthorized distribution that could lead to uncontrolled use and potential safety issues; and, an environmental negative impact where AureoBos is not disposed correctly. Each of these risks, as well as some other legal considerations that directly impact the development of our product, is further detailed and addressed in our legal strategy which you can find on our entrepreneurship section.

Mastitis knows no borders—it's a challenge uniting dairy farmers worldwide. With our project, we aim to break these boundaries and make a global impact. Successfully implementing our solution will empower farmers and transform dairy enterprises across the globe, one cow at a time.
