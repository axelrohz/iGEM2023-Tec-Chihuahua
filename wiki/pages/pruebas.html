How we started and why Chihuahua?

In the biggest state of Mexico, Chihuahua, lies our tight-knit team, bound not only by our passion for science but also by our deep love for our community. Each one of us has a unique connection to this land, whether we were born here or have spent most of our lives embracing its breathtaking landscapes and basking in the warmth of its people. This beautiful state thrives on its economy driven by mining, agriculture, and livestock sectors. The determination and hard work of its people has been the lifeblood of our local economy, shaping the fundamental structure of our society.


In the early stages of our project, we pondered the possible challenges to tackle, and one of our teammates, Alexa, shared a story about the issues her godmother, Marcela Hernández, a woman who owns a dairy farm, faced. This sparked our curiosity, and we embarked on an online investigation into the world of dairy farming. During our research, a pressing issue captured our attention – mastitis, a prevalent problem in the dairy industry. We became aware of the significant financial losses that this disease caused for dairy cattle owners, a revelation that awakened us to the gravity of the issue, and we felt compelled to explore this issue further. We were also motivated by the importance of the dairy industry in our home state of Chihuahua. Chihuahua is a major milk producer in Mexico, ranking fourth in milk production in 2022[1], and is currently third in terms of dairy cattle population. Milk is a nutrient-dense food that plays an important role in the diet of over 6 billion people around the world. It provides high-quality protein and essential micronutrients in an easily absorbed form[2].


In fact, 82% of total world milk is obtained from cattle[3], and its production has increased more than 59% in the last three decades, from 530 million tons in 1988 to 843 million tons in 2018. About 150 million households worldwide are engaged in dairy farming. In most developing countries, milk production is carried out by smallholders, exerting a significant impact on household livelihoods, food security and nutritional requirements. For these smallholders dairy farming offers a crucial source of income[4].


We believe that our project has the potential to make a significant impact, not just within Chihuahua’s dairy sector, but also on a global scale across the dairy industry. By developing a treatment for mastitis, we can help to reduce the financial losses caused by this disease, improve the health of dairy cows, and help reduce milk loss. This will make a difference for dairy farmers in Chihuahua and around the globe. For the most part, it will help to ensure that milk remains as a safe and affordable source of nutrients for people all around the world.
