Safety

Safety first!

Overview

In the ongoing project, genetically modified organisms (GMOs) are employed to produce endolysins as an alternative to antibiotics, offering a novel approach to combat diseases and tackle antibiotic resistance. Within the laboratory context, maintaining safety protocols is of utmost importance. Stringent adherence to Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) utilization, Risk Group containment, Good Laboratory Practices (GLP), and Biosafety Levels are vital to prevent unintended exposure to GMOs and endolysins. The laboratory infrastructure is designed to minimize potential risks associated with working with genetically modified organisms and to prevent potential release into the environment. By prioritizing robust safety measures, the research team aims to safeguard laboratory personnel and the broader community.

Safety guidelines

Personal protective equipment

Disposable personal protective equipment (PPE), such as nitrile gloves, was appropriately disposed of, and non-disposable items like lab coats were stored in their designated location. Adhering to the PPE guidelines, non-disposable equipment remained within its designated area, avoiding potential risks to team members and the campus community. Personal items that could contaminate, like necklaces and earrings, were not worn. The correct use of PPE was strictly upheld throughout laboratory activities.

Safe spaces

To ensure biological safety, the laboratory workspace was kept clear at all times, and measures were taken to prevent the use of items that could pose a potential biological risk, including earrings, rings, and bracelets. For this purpose, a designated area outside the laboratory was provided for storing personal belongings such as jewelry, bracelets, caps, and backpacks. 1

Bacterial cultures and any associated debris were meticulously disposed of following the addition of sodium hypochlorite, a potent disinfectant, to neutralize any potential hazards. This conscientious approach prevented the risk of contamination and the unintentional spread of microorganisms beyond our controlled environment. 2

Additionally, our team made sure safety came first by making sure all emergency exit doors were clear and easy to use. This careful planning helps evacuation go smoothly if something unexpected happens, like accidents or fires. These actions show how committed we are to creating a safe lab space. We're not just focused on doing experiments well; we also want to create a culture where we're always watching out for potential issues and ready to handle anything that comes up.3

Access

As previously mentioned, we operated within Level 2 containment laboratories, which are accessible only to individuals affiliated with the field of biotechnology. Access is limited to biotechnology faculty members and alumni, and students are granted entry only after successfully completing safety training.

Waste Disposal

Laboratory waste generally falls into four categories, each with its own disposal method. Proper disposal is crucial to prevent contamination and accidents. Solid biological waste goes into a yellow bag, while non-solid biological waste is placed in a sealed yellow bottle. Sharp objects and used electrophoresis gels are disposed of in a red plastic bottle. Any materials in contact with bacteria or other infectious agents are put into a red bag. Given our project involving bacteria, we mainly used red containers like bags and bottles for disposal.
