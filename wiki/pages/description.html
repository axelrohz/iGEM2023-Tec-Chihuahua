Food Safety Starts On The Farm



AureoBos’ Origin

In the biggest state of Mexico, Chihuahua, lies our tight-knit team, bound not only by our passion for science but also by our love for our community. Our connection to the region, rich in stunning landscapes and warm people, drives our work. The state's economy thrives on mining, agriculture, and livestock activities, with the determination of its residents shaping society's core. During the project's early stages, inspired by a story from our teammate Alexa about her godmother's dairy farm challenges, we delved into dairy farming issues. Mastitis, a prevalent disease affecting dairy cattle, caught our attention due to its significant multifaceted impact on dairy owners. Motivated by Chihuahua's prominence in the Mexican dairy industry [1], we recognized the global significance of addressing this issue.


Analyzing the problems that impact the world, we sought to understand how our community is bound to the international economic activities, and we found the perfect candidate: milk. Milk is a nutrient-dense food that plays an important role in the diet of over 6 billion people around the world. It provides high-quality protein and essential micronutrients in an easily absorbed form (figure 1) [2].


We aim to contribute to our feeding’s backbone by offering a safe solution to one of the biggest problems of the milk production chain. With a shared commitment, we work in an attempt to reduce food waste and improve quality as well as enhance food safety . This way, AureoBos reveals a new possibility to revolutionize the dairy industry.


This will make a difference for dairy farmers in Chihuahua and around the globe.

Into the Udder / Impact of Bovine Mastitis


When we think about milk, we immediately associate it with cows, and that is not very far from reality.


In fact, 82% of total world milk is obtained from cattle [3]. About 150 million households worldwide are engaged in dairy farming. In most developing countries, like Mexico, milk production is carried out by smallholders, exerting a significant impact on household livelihoods, food security and nutritional requirements. For these smallholders, dairy farming offers a crucial source of income [4].


Did You Know…


With an astonishing production of 550,498 thousand metric tons of milk as of July 2023 [5], the dairy industry stands a major employer worldwide, underscoring the critical importance of udder health.


The low affordability and availability of milk are related to various factors like the deficient cattle facilities and milking procedures, which can lead to mastitis [6], an inflammation of the mammary gland caused by several mistakes. (Image 1) [7]. (Añadir diagrama de las causas de mastitis con su respectiva fuente) The disease’s occurrence may also be influenced by host factors (figure 3).


Certain contagious pathogens stand out as significant contributors, notably Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus agalactiae, and Streptococcus uberis (figure 2), all of which damage the milking tissue, thus, causing irreversible damage to the cow’s milk production.

S. aureus, the predominant pathogen responsible for bovine mastitis, produces harmful toxins and enzymes. These include protein A, which hinders antibody recognition by neutrophils; beta-lactamase inducingmicroabscess formation; and TSST-1 (Toxic shock syndrome toxin-1), the major cause of toxic shock syndrome characterized by fever, rash and organ failure [8] [9].


On the other hand, S. agalactiae is adept at forming biofilms and resides in the bovine gastrointestinal tract [10]. Lastly, S. uberis is associated with clinical and sub-clinical infections. It was reported that α-casein and β-casein in milk induce production of biofilms, which help S. uberis to persist under environmental stress and resist antibiotic treatment [11].


The impact of bovine mastitis extends beyond individual cows. When engaging with our stakeholders, we noticed that the absence of preventive measures such as proper hygiene in both the cowpens and the milking routine significantly raises the risk of infection. Mastitis infections manifest in three forms: subclinical, clinical, and chronic (figure 4). Subclinical mastitis, if not addressed, progresses to clinical stage. Untreated mastitis lowers milk production, increases financial losses, and can harm the cow's udder. Severe cases can even result in cow mortality [12].
