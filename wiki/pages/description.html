Healthy Udders, no antibiotics: The Power of endolysins

Please see the 2023 Medals Page for more information.

What is it about?

    Bacteriophage endolysins are considered a great alternative for antibiotics since they have shown specific antimicrobial activity against gram positive bacteria, even in antibiotic resistant strains.Using the power of endolysins, we approach the bovine mastitis problem caused by two gram-positive bacterias: Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus agalactiae. Endolysins interact with the bacterial cell membrane, causing cellular lysis. These proteins are composed of two main domains: N-terminal part, which presents the catalytic domain, and C-terminal part, which is the cell wall binding domain. This last domain determines the specificity of the endolysin against bacterias (Fernández et al., 2017). Nonetheless, the endolysins half-life approaches 20 minutes. For this reason, we look forward to increasing its half-life by binding the protein to albumin. In this project we use two endolysins: LysCSA13 and LysK.

Problem Description

into the udder

Bovine mastitis is the most common disease in dairy cattle, which consists in the inflammation of the udders mammary gland caused due to microorganism infection (Cheng & Han, 2020).This can have multiple causes, such as poor environmental hygiene, bad milking habits and other management practices (Cheng & Han, 2020). Bovine mastitis is classified in three classes, based on the inflammation: clinical, subclinical and chronic. The first one is easily detected, since it presents a red and swollen udder, as well as fever. The milk appears watery with the presence of flakes and clots. On the other side, subclinical mastitis has no visible abnormalities, but the milk production decreases and the somatic cell count increases. Lastly, chronic mastitis is a major problem since it lasts for several months and it presents clinical flare-ups at irregular intervals. Among the numerous etiological agents, Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus agalactiae are considered contagious. Staphylococcus aureus is the most prevailing pathogen known to cause bovine mastitis. This bacteria produces toxins and enzymes that damage the milking tissue. On the other hand, Streptococcus agalactiae produces biofilms and it is found in the bovine gastrointestinal tract. This disease has multiple host factors such as age, breeding and genetic and udder structure (Cheng & Han, 2020).

Milk in the world

Bovine mastitis is one of the main causes of economic loss in the Mexican dairy industry. In addition to losses in milk production, this means it is not a separate consequence generated by the disease, it is a cause-effect relationship (you have less milk/lower quality, that affects you economically and affects dairy products)

Beating mastitis

The most used treatments against mastitis include intramammary injection, dry cow therapy, and vaccination. Still, the most common treatment for mastitis in cattle is the use of antibiotics. The Mexican government approves the use of penicillin, ceftiofur, and gentamicin for the treatment of mastitis in cattle. Other treatments that are available in Mexico include bacteriophages, probiotics, and herbal remedies.According to a recent study by the World Organization for Animal Health, Mexico ranks 37th in the world in terms of the availability of treatments for mastitis in cattle. Chihuahua ranks 19th in Mexico in terms of the availability of treatments for mastitis in cattle.Due to their efectivity, antibiotics continue to be one of the chosen techniques among cattle owners. Thus, significant portions of antibiotics are released through milk and exert serious harmful effects on human health. (Sachi et al., 2019)In many countries, there are strict regulations on the use of antibiotics in dairy cows. After administering an antibiotic to a cow, a specific period of time, known as the withdrawal period, must be respected before the milk from that cow can be collected for human consumption. This means that during that period the milk is discarded, which can cause economic losses for the producer.Among all the treatments, intramammary infusion of antibiotics has been shown to be effective in reducing the incidence of mastitis and improving milk quality. Other treatments, such as bacteriophages, have not been shown to be as effective.

The risk beyond

Antibiotics are substances used to fight against bacterial infections. Sometimes, bacteria show resistance to these substances. While this feature can be innate or acquired, plasmid-induced resistance is responsible for most of the resistance spread. Also, biofilm growth has shown to preserve bacterias from antibiotic treatment.Therefore, if not administered correctly, antibiotic use can result counter-productive. (Palma et al., 2020)Antimicrobial resistance is something inevitable, but the situation we are facing is the consequence of disproportionate and mistaken usage of antibiotics. Mainly, antimicrobial resistance presents a problem to the health sector, but it has been shown that also the economy is affected: the World Bank estimated that up to 3.8% of the global gross domestic product could be lost due to antimicrobial resistance by 2050. According to Center for Disease Control (CDC’s) Antibiotic Resistance Threats Report 2019 in the United States, more than 2.8 million antibiotic-resistant infections arise in the United States per year (Uddin et al., 2021)One useful tool to record antimicrobial resistance progress and action plans is the Global Antimicrobial Resistance and Use Surveillance System. Currently, 109 countries form part of this organization. Mexico is not one of them. According to GLASS report, antimicrobial resistance is among the top 10 global health threats, since recent studies show that it is one of the main causes of death worldwide. It is estimated that 4·95 million deaths were associated with bacterial antibiotic resistance in 2019. Nowadays, 90% of countries have developed a national action plan regarding this problem, but only 20% have identified funding for implementing and monitoring these plans. Higher antimicrobial resistance rates have been documented in several low- and middle-income countries. Still, detecting antimicrobial resistance in bacteria can be a hard job, since diagnostic practices are different in every country, depending on the economic situation and awareness. (GLASS, 2022)
