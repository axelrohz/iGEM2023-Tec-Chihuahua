Use of bacteriophage endolysins to offer a nonantibiotic solution to contagious bovine mastitis caused by Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus agalactiae

Healthy Udders, no antibiotics: The Power of endolysins

What is it about?

    Bovine mastitis is a disease that affects the dairy industry, from economic loss to quality impoverishment .Conventionally, this is treated with antibiotics which present a quick and effective solution. However, the excessive use of these substances has led to a global problematic: antibiotic resistance. To reduce this, it is imperative to stop antibiotic overuse and start looking out for other solutions, which in our case are endolysins.Bacteriophage endolysins are considered a great alternative for antibiotics since they have shown specific antimicrobial activity against gram positive bacteria, even in antibiotic resistant strains.Using the power of endolysins, we approach the bovine mastitis problem caused by two gram-positive bacterias: Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus agalactiae. Endolysins interact with the bacterial cell membrane, causing cellular lysis. These proteins have two ends: N-terminus, the catalytic domain, and C-terminus, the cell wall binding domain, which determines the specificity of the endolysin against bacterias (Fernández et al., 2017). Nonetheless, the endolysin’s half-life is around 20 minutes (Resch et al., 2011). For this reason, we look forward to increasing its half-life by binding the protein to albumin binding domain (Seijsing et al., 2018b). In this project we use two endolysins: LysCSA13 and LysK.

Problem Description

Into the udder

Bovine mastitis is the most common disease in dairy cattle (Cheng & Han, 2020), which consists in the inflammation of the udder’s mammary gland caused due to microorganism infection . This can have multiple causes, such as poor environmental hygiene, bad milking habits and other management practices (Sharun et al., 2021).Bovine mastitis is classified in three classes, based on the inflammation: clinical, subclinical and chronic. The first one is easily detected, since it presents a red and swollen udder, as well as fever. The milk appears watery with the presence of flakes and clots. On the other side, subclinical mastitis has no visible abnormalities, but the milk production decreases and the somatic cell count increases. Lastly, chronic mastitis is a major problem since it lasts for several months and it presents clinical flare-ups at irregular intervals. (Cheng & Han, 2020). Among the numerous etiological agents, S. aureus and S. agalactiae are considered contagious. S. aureus is the most prevailing pathogen known to cause bovine mastitis. This bacteria produces toxins and enzymes that damage the milking tissue. On the other hand, S. agalactiae produces biofilms and it is found in the bovine gastrointestinal tract. This disease has multiple host factors such as age, breeding and genetic and udder structure (Cheng & Han, 2020).

Milk in the world

Mastitis causes an increase of costs, from treatment to rearing, when the milk production is affected. Also, it negatively impacts the composition, quantity and quality of the milk, as well as affected cows face the aftermath: damaged udders, affecting milk production in the future (Sharun et al., 2021). For this reason, bovine mastitis is one of the main causes of economic loss in the Mexican dairy industry, as well as worldwide (Quintana, 2021).

Given the rising numbers of dairy cattle, the incidence of this disease also rises. This is why it is estimated that by 2027 the bovine mastitis market size increases, contributing importantly to the global economy.

Beating mastitis

The most used treatments against mastitis include intramammary injection, dry cow therapy, and vaccination. Still, the most common treatment for mastitis in cattle is the use of antibiotics. The Mexican government approves the use of penicillin, ceftiofur, and gentamicin for the treatment of mastitis in cattle. Other treatments that are available in Mexico include bacteriophages, probiotics, and herbal remedies.According to a recent study by the World Organization for Animal Health, Mexico ranks 37th in the world in terms of the availability of treatments for mastitis in cattle. Chihuahua ranks 19th in Mexico in terms of the availability of treatments for mastitis in cattle.Due to their efectivity, antibiotics continue to be one of the chosen techniques among cattle owners. Thus, significant portions of antibiotics are released through milk and exert serious harmful effects on human health. (Sachi et al., 2019)In many countries, there are strict regulations on the use of antibiotics in dairy cows. After administering an antibiotic to a cow, a specific period of time, known as the withdrawal period, must be respected before the milk from that cow can be collected for human consumption. This means that during that period the milk is discarded, which can cause economic losses for the producer.Among all the treatments, intramammary infusion of antibiotics has been shown to be effective in reducing the incidence of mastitis and improving milk quality. Other treatments, such as bacteriophages, have not been shown to be as effective.

The risk beyond

Antibiotics are substances used to fight against bacterial infections, being this case mastitis. It is important to mention that sometimes, bacteria show resistance to these substances. While this feature can be innate or acquired, plasmid-induced resistance is responsible for most of the resistance spread. Also, biofilm growth has shown to preserve bacterias from antibiotic treatment. Therefore, if not administered correctly, antibiotic use can result counter-productive because it can cause antibiotic resistance. (Palma et al., 2020) Antimicrobial resistance is something inevitable, but the situation we are facing is the consequence of disproportionate and mistaken usage of antibiotics. Mainly, antimicrobial resistance presents a problem to the health sector, but it has been shown that also the economy is affected: the World Bank estimated that up to 3.8% of the global gross domestic product could be lost due to antimicrobial resistance by 2050 (World Bank, 2017).According to Center for Disease Control (CDC’s) Antibiotic Resistance Threats Report 2019 in the United States, more than 2.8 million antibiotic-resistant infections arise in the United States per year (Uddin et al., 2021). In addition, ccording to GLASS’ report, antimicrobial resistance is among the top 10 global health threats, since it is estimated that 4·95 million deaths were associated with bacterial antibiotic resistance in 2019. For this reason, it is important to start becoming aware of the problem that antibiotics in our food represent.
